# Stratification Process
Stratification itself is a straightforward process. You can select one or two strata to put pairs into. For example, you could choose to place all Bronze pairs (that is, 5-star Senior Master or below) into a stratum. This results in two sets of rankings:

* The overall rankings - i.e. all pairs
* The rankings only for Bronze pairs

Each of these rankings is awarded masterpoints in accordance with the current SBU masterpoints awards scheme. The masterpoint awards in the overall rankings are then updated to include any masterpoint awards gained by the lower stratum. A pair in a lower stratum that gained masterpoints in both the overall rankings and in their stratum receives the higher of the two awards (not both).

**This does not affect the score that a pair achieved - only the masterpoints awards.**

Unless you have a particularly large field, you will normally only use one stratum below the overall rankings.

The strata are named A, B and C.

* A holds the overall ranking and includes the whole field.
* B holds all pairs equal to, or below, the rank you select for stratum B
* C holds all pairs equal to, or below, the rank you select for stratum C. This level must be lower than that selected for stratum B.

So, stratum A holds all pairs, stratum B holds all stratum B **and** stratum C pairs and stratum C holds only stratum C pairs.

## Masterpoint Awards

Masterpoints are awarded at a slightly reduced rate than if the results were not stratified.

Pairs ranked above Stratum B may feel a little aggrieved by this, since they are "losing out", but it's done this way to keep the total masterpoints awarded in a stratified event comparable to the total awarded if the event was not stratified.

Effectively, some of the masterpoints awarded to the top places in the overall rankings are donated to the lower strata.

## Stratum Rankings

As mentioned above, you may choose one or two strata (B and C). Selecting only a single stratum (B) places all pairs below (and including) the selected rank into a separate ranking set. Masterpoints are then awarded at a reduced rate to that ranking set as if this was a separate tournament (you may think of this as a tournament within a tournament).

If a pair achieves masterpoints in both the overall ranking and any strata in which they are placed, they earn whichever is the largest award. They do not get the sum of their awards from all strata!

For example, if you wished to have a single stratum for Bronze pairs only, you would select "5 Star Senior Master" as the stratum level for B and not configure stratum C.
This will produce an overall ranking and a separate, stratified ranking for all Bronze pairs.

In a large enough field, you may choose to have 2 strata by selecting a value for stratum C. You cannot select a rank for stratum C that is higher or the same as the stratum B rank.

This will result in an overall ranking, a ranking of all pairs at or below the stratum B threshold and a ranking of all pairs at or below the stratum C threshold.

## Differing Ranks

Where two players in a pair have different masterpoint ranks, the **highest masterpoints rank** in this pairing is used to decide which stratum the pair should be in. So, if a Scottish Master plays with a District Master partner, the pair is treated as being Scottish Master for the purposes of stratification.

## Default Rankings

The [Options](userif.md#options) settings allow you to set up the default rankings used for stratification. Most clubs will set this up for the most common case. You can change the strata rankings for a competition without affecting the default rankings.

## Masterpoint Scales

As per non-stratified events, the number of masterpoints awarded depends on the number of pairs competing. Therefore stratification is only worthwhile if there are a sufficient number of pairs in a stratum for masterpoints to be awarded.

That said, you can still use the stratification process to divide up the field by rank for the purposes of showing pairs how they performed with respect to other pairs of similar rank. This only applies to printed output or the stand-alone webpage.

The SBU Masterpoints and Licensing Handbook sets out the masterpoint award scales. Section 4.3 defines that there must be **at least 3 complete tables for a one-winner event** and **at least 4 complete tables for a two-winner event**. If there are fewer than these numbers in a stratum, no masterpoints are awarded in that stratum.

**If no stratified masterpoints are awarded at all, the original masterpoints remain unchanged.**

In a non-stratified event, masterpoints are only awarded if all pairs have played at least 12 boards (the top quarter are awarded) and if all pairs have played at least 18 boards, the top third are awarded masterpoints. However, in a stratified event, **all pairs must have played at least 18 boards for stratification to be permitted**.

As mentioned earlier, the scale of awards in a stratified tournament is reduced slightly. Compared to a non-stratified tournament, this reduces the masterpoints awarded to the winners in the overall results at the expense of awarding some masterpoints to lower placed pairs that win awards in their stratum.

This means that the total masterpoints awarded to a stratified event may be slightly larger or smaller than before stratification, but not significantly so.

At the present time, the program only awards LOCAL points, according to the scales defined in the SBU Masterpoints and Licensing Handbook.

Awards are made to the top proportion of the field as normal (the top third of the field, rounded up). This applies both to the overall rankings and to any stratum.

For example, if there were 16 pairs in a single winner event, the top 5 pairs in the overall ranking achieve awards. If the tournament was stratified to one stratum, with 6 pairs in that stratum, then the top 2 pairs in that stratum would also be entitled to awards.
